!Sample_growth_protocol_ch1 = The human cell line Panc89 (kindly provided by Prof. T. Okabe, University of Tokyo, JP) originating from a lymph node metastasis of PDAC was used as a model for epithelial PDAC cells. As a model for mesenchymal PDAC cells, the human cell line Panc1 was used (purchased from ATCC, Manassas, Virginia US) originating from a primary tumor of a PDAC patient. Single cell cloning of Panc1 cells to isolate Panc1 Holo- and Paraclone cells, respectively, is described in Knaack et. al., Oncotarget 2018. To isolate Holo- and Paraclone cells from Panc89 cells, the parental cells were pre-diluted to a cell count of 1000 cells per ml Panc-medium. From this cell suspension, 100 µl (corre-sponding to 100 cells) were added to 20 ml of Panc-medium to obtain a concentration of one cell per 200 µl of cell medium, which were filled in each well of a flat 96-well plate. The plate was centrifuged and the first screening was performed directly after centrifuga-tion by imaging the plate in the brightfield channel of a NYONE® Scientific Imager (Synen-Tec GmbH, Elmshorn, DE) and with the brightfield channel of EVOS microscope (AMG, Bothell, US). Only wells containing exactly one cell were considered for further monitoring and expansion. The microscopical screening was performed twice a week. After 12-20 days, the colony shape was determined and the colonies monitored until a cell confluence of about 80%. Cell clones with a stable colony morphology were detached from one well and transferred to one well of a 6-well plate. The morphology of expanded cell clones was con-tinued to be checked regularly. Only clones with a clear CSC- or non-CSC morphology and stable phenotype were further cultivated, expanded and used for phenotypic and functional characterization. Phenotypes were regularly checked by marker analysis via qPCR.
