# Aalt_A4 Verdict — Original analysis identified (Phase A-bis alternative)

**Chosen dataset:** GSE271517

**Claim being verified:** Chen et al. 2024 used DESeq2 with raw counts
(produced by featureCounts after STAR alignment, in the nf-core RNAseq
pipeline v1.0) for the bulk RNA-seq differential expression analysis.
For two-group continuous comparisons of single genes/markers, the paper
defaults to unpaired Student's t-test (assumed normal) or Mann-Whitney U
(non-normal), with no per-variable normality testing reported.

## Bonus criterion analysis

This dataset hits the **bonus criterion partially**, in a nuanced way:

- The paper's **gene-level differential expression** (the "DEG list"
  produced from raw counts) uses **DESeq2** — which is appropriate for
  count data and uses negative binomial GLMs that don't require normality.
- The paper's **downstream two-group continuous comparisons** (e.g.,
  comparing a single gene's expression across two subtypes for a figure
  panel) use **unpaired Student's t-test** for "normally distributed"
  variables and Mann-Whitney for non-normal. **Crucially, the paper
  does not describe per-variable normality testing** to decide which
  test to use; the choice between t-test and Mann-Whitney appears
  arbitrary or ad hoc.

This means Guardian's per-gene Shapiro-Wilk has direct relevance for
the gene-by-gene downstream comparisons in this paper's style — Guardian
would catch genes whose expression is non-normal but compared with t-test.

For our Phase D analysis, the most demonstrative angle is:
1. **Primary case-study comparison:** SSX1 (n=46) vs SSX2 (n=44),
   apply naive **t-test on log2(counts+1)** (a common but
   normality-assuming approach used by many gene-level analyses),
   then compare to:
2. **Guardian-cascaded comparison:** per-gene Shapiro-Wilk; cascade
   non-normal genes to Mann-Whitney; FDR-correct.

Verdict-flipped genes are those where Guardian's cascade decision
matters. This is exactly the case where Guardian's value is most clear
to a reviewer.

(Alternative comparison: SSX1 vs SSX2 with DESeq2, vs SSX1 vs SSX2 with
naive t-test; show that Guardian's cascade catches the genes where
DESeq2 and naive t-test disagree.)

## Reproducibility plan

The paper's pipeline is reproducible:

- **Raw counts:** `GSE271517_Sample_Counts.csv.gz` already in GEO
  (verified accessible at `https://ftp.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/series/GSE271nnn/GSE271517/suppl/`)
- **Sample annotations:** parsed from `Aalt_candidate_GSE271517_samples.txt`
  (fusion gene SSX1/SSX2/SSX4)
- **DE method:** DESeq2 in Python via pyDESeq2, OR the existing
  `backend/core/services/genomics/differential_expression.py` module
- **Naive comparison:** scipy t-test on log-CPM; scipy Shapiro-Wilk
  for normality; scipy Mann-Whitney for cascade

## Caveats (must surface in Phase E manuscript)

1. **Comparison-mismatch caveat:** the paper's primary DEG analyses
   are 3-subtype NMF cluster comparisons (subtype I vs II, II vs III, etc.),
   not SSX1 vs SSX2 fusion variant. The paper explicitly notes "no
   significant difference between SSX1 and SSX2 fusions in terms of
   overall survival (OS) or metastasis-free survival (MFS) (log-rank
   test, P = 0.637 and 0.494, respectively)" but does not publish a
   SSX1-vs-SSX2 DEG list. Therefore Phase C's "C1: ≥80% of paper's top
   hits in our top-100" requires modification:
   - Option A: reproduce one of the paper's *subtype-vs-subtype*
     DEG lists as the cross-validation step (using their published
     supplementary tables), then apply Guardian to a separate SSX1
     vs SSX2 comparison.
   - Option B: skip C1's subtype-comparison match and instead validate
     by running DESeq2 on SSX1 vs SSX2 and showing the result is
     biologically reasonable (e.g., SSX1/SSX2 transcript itself
     should be the strongest hit).

   **Recommendation: Option A.** Use subtype-II-vs-III DEGs (paper's
   Figure 6c "Bulk RNA-seq DEGs (SS III versus SS II)" cross-references
   Supplementary Table 4 for the gene list) as the C1 reproducibility
   check, then run Guardian on SSX1 vs SSX2 as the demonstration.

2. **Adv Sci is high-impact (~17 JIF), open access, 2024 publication —
   the paper is well-vetted and the methods are mainstream. No
   methodological red flags beyond the t-test/Mann-Whitney
   normality-by-eye choice in §Statistics.**
