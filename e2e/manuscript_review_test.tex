\section{Results}

We compared the two groups using an independent-samples $t$-test. Group A
($M = 12.4$, $SD = 3.1$) scored higher than Group B ($M = 10.1$, $SD = 2.9$),
and the difference was statistically significant, $t(58) = 2.01$, $p = 0.049$.

A one-way ANOVA across the three conditions revealed a significant main effect,
$F(2, 87) = 3.15$, $p = 0.001$.

The association between treatment and outcome was assessed with a chi-square test
and was highly significant, $\chi^2(1) = 3.84$, $p = 0.001$.

Reaction time was correlated with accuracy, $r(48) = 0.35$, $p = 0.013$.

Finally, the treatment effect did not reach statistical significance,
$t(40) = 2.30$, $p = 0.027$.
